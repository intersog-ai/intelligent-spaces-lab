In season one, the home keeps two things apart: what it observed, and what that might mean. The home reports. A person, or the monitoring centre, decides. For smoke, the centre calls nine one one. The home never does.

Today we build that in a digital twin. The code and the data are free. It is provided as is, and it is not a certified alarm system. The cellular link, the camera detector and the people in it are models, and every assumption is written down.

First, a gap we left open. In episodes one and four, with the internet down, an alarm reached the monitoring centre only when the internet came back. Four and a half minutes in one test. Ten hours in another.

So we added a second path: a modelled cellular link, with its own delay, losses and cost. Both paths send heartbeats, so the hub and the centre know which one works. If an alarm is not acknowledged in five seconds, the next try goes on the other path, at once.
