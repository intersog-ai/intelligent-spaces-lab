In season one, episode three, someone says: we're leaving. Lights go off, the heating drops, the shades close, and the home checks a window. One shade does not respond. And the home says so. Today we build that over a real protocol, on one machine, with no cloud in the path.

This is research. The broker and the messages are real. The devices are still our twin, and there are no radios. The code and the data are free to download. It is provided as is.

We ran a real MQTT broker on the same computer as the hub. Every device got its own connection, and its own last will: a message the broker sends for it if it disappears. Every device also published configuration topics that Home Assistant can discover.

First number. A message crossed the broker in about half a millisecond. The whole routine took eight to nine seconds, because that is how long a shade takes to move. Locally, the protocol is not the slow part.

Then we cut the power to the bedroom shade, just before the routine. No goodbye, no error. Silence. The broker noticed after fifteen seconds, one and a half times the keepalive interval, in forty eight trials out of forty eight. A crashed program was noticed in a hundredth of a second. Waiting for missed heartbeats would have taken about two minutes.

So the home did not say done. It said: five of six steps confirmed. The bedroom shade went offline and could not act, so it may still be open. Check it when you can. And the kitchen window is open. I can't close it. Someone needs to.

Lesson one. Silence is not a state. The last will turns silence into a fact, but only as fast as the keepalive you choose.

Lesson two. Quality of service one does not fix a lossy radio. We dropped up to twenty percent of messages before they reached the broker. The protocol never saw them. What recovered the commands was our own code: send the same command again, and believe only the device's state.

Even then, at twenty percent loss, four steps out of eighty really happened, but their final report was lost. The home said: it received the command, I have no reading that it finished. Not done. Not failed. Unknown, and it will keep checking.

Lesson three. Quality of service one does work across a reconnect. A command sent during a network blip arrived three times out of three. Then we allowed anonymous clients, one setting in the broker, and it arrived zero times out of three. No error anywhere.

Lesson four. Test the broker, not only your code. Ours sent clients retained messages they never subscribed to. And our own hub crashed on an empty message, which is how MQTT deletes one. Both are fixed or fenced, and written down.

What we did not run: Matter and Thread on real radios. We mapped every device to Matter clusters, checked against the official definitions, and found one trap. Matter counts a shade by how closed it is. Home Assistant counts how open.

Everything is in the repository, with the commands to run it in a little over three minutes. If you build devices, platforms or spaces like these, Intersog's engineers do this work.
